From buildings to systems

Architecture and AI systems design look like different disciplines. In practice, they share the same underlying problem: you are designing something complex that will be used by people who didn't build it, in conditions you can't fully predict. The methodology transfers.

Architecture
Understand the place before touching it. Map what already exists — social, ecological, historical. Build from what's real, not from an abstract brief. Design for the whole system, not just the object.
What transfers
Reading complex systems. Identifying where the real constraint is. Distinguishing a symptom from a root cause. Designing for failure modes, not just for ideal use. Building things that can be maintained by others.
Systems design
Understand the problem before writing code. Map the failure modes. Encode the rules that must hold as hard constraints — not suggestions, not preferences. Build instruments that behave the same way under pressure as they do in testing.

What I've built

Four instruments across completely unrelated fields — scientific research, AI governance, music, and civic planning. Each was built from scratch, in a domain I had no prior technical experience in. Each one produced the same underlying pattern: when you encode the rules that must hold as hard constraints rather than learned preferences, the system becomes measurably more robust, more auditable, and more trustworthy.

Community Planning Support System (CPSS)
A constitutional AI platform that puts 54 live UK government data feeds into the hands of community groups fighting planning applications. Enter a postcode, and the system instantly maps Green Belt status, flood zones, protected habitats, environmental deprivation, and planning history — then cross-references every constraint against the exact NPPF paragraph or Act section it triggers. A built-in document library reads developer submissions and extracts the numbers that matter. A circulation tool sends briefings to your list. The next release adds the full statutory objection letter — passed through a four-validator forensic check for factual accuracy, planning-law compliance, internal consistency, and tone before it reaches you.
Live at yourplanningvoice.com. Deploying with the Community Planning Alliance (800+ UK community organisations). Independent legal verification on live cases: 88–93% objection strength. Crowdfunding target: £30,000. National Lottery Community Fund application in preparation.

Darwinian Novelty Indicator (DNI v6.0)
A retrieval-augmented, multi-judge system that measures whether a scientific paper is genuinely novel. It reads the full manuscript, retrieves the actual cited references from OpenAlex, Crossref, and Semantic Scholar in real time, and runs a panel of 5–10 adversarial AI judges — each with independently mutated weights — across four dimensions: Uniqueness, Tension, Synthesis, and Coherence. Disagreement triggers a second panel rather than being averaged away. A hardcoded integrity layer — the Review Cap, the epistemic groundedness screen, and the Coherence Veto — corrects the output in code, not in prompts. Think of it as a thermometer, not a judge: the LLM measures against a scale; it doesn't get to decide what "novel" means.
Special Mention, UKRI Metascience Novelty Indicators Challenge. Highest individual metric score on the Liu & Zhai (2026) axiomatic benchmark (73.0% overall; 55.1% on the hardest axiom vs. the previous best of 6%). Piloting with the Research on Research Institute (RoRI) for integration into their Funder Data Platform. Calibration study with SPRU, University of Sussex, in development. Published on the RoRI Substack; technical companion on Zenodo.

AURA-ECHO
A neurosymbolic music system — built as a test environment for the constitutional AI architecture — that generates music under adversarial conditions. The interesting result wasn't the music. It was the behaviour under attack: 319 sessions of deliberate attempts to break the system's governing rules, zero successes. The system doesn't just refuse bad instructions; it detects when the load on its decision-making is being artificially inflated and responds before a violation can occur.
Produced the empirical validation of the Constitutional Physics framework. Co-authored preprint with Dr. Tomas Veloz (Vrije Universiteit Brussel). Endorsed by Audrey Tang, former Digital Minister of Taiwan. 1,800+ preprint downloads.

AEOLUS — Constitutional AI for Net‑Zero Planning
A neuro‑symbolic platform that generates legally compliant, physically grounded decarbonisation and flexibility plans for public‑sector buildings. An LLM proposes, but five deterministic engines (energy, building physics, biodiversity, climate risk, finance) and a panel of constitutional judges verify every output before it reaches the user. The system cannot converge on a hallucination.
Validated on four real, unseen public‑sector baselines — an NHS Trust, a London borough’s housing stock, and two university campuses — producing investment‑grade, regulation‑compliant blueprints in under three minutes each. Combined results: 32,334 tCO₂e annual reductions, £8.9 M annual savings, +£8.4 M NPV. Currently being extended for real‑time consumer‑led flexibility (Innovate UK CLF competition application submitted).

OIKOS — Passivhaus Compliance Verification Engine
A neuro‑symbolic system that reads architectural documents, extracts physical parameters, and checks them against the deterministic Passivhaus numeric thresholds across seven climate zones and five certification pathways. The LLM never decides compliance — a hard‑coded genotype makes every determination, and five expert AI personas review the results for physical plausibility, buildability risk, and documentation completeness.
Built and type‑checked across 11 document types and 12 criteria. Successfully verified a PHI‑published Passivhaus example on its first run with 10/10 extraction accuracy. Awaiting real‑world messy‑document testing.

Current focus — Community Planning Support System

The CPSS is the most concrete application of the methodology to date. It takes a technical architecture developed across AI safety research and applies it to one of the most practically urgent asymmetries in British civic life.

"Community groups know their place better than any consultant. They just can't express what they know in the form the planning system requires."

When a developer applies to build on a flood plain or a peat bog or a community park, they arrive with a professional legal team, environmental surveys, and planning statements that cite the right policy paragraphs. The community group arrives with local knowledge, genuine concern, and a deadline measured in weeks.

CPSS doesn't replace the community's knowledge — it translates it. You tell the system what you know about your place. It cross-references that against 54 government data feeds, checks it against relevant case law, and produces a letter in the statutory language that a planning officer is required to consider. The letter is verifiable: every claim it makes is traceable back to a real data source or a real legal precedent.

The proof of concept is complete. We are now preparing for the next stage: expanding the community partnership network, securing funding, and moving toward a governed, community-owned service.
